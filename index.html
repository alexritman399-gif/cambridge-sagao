Academic Reading · Passage 1

Urban green spaces and public health

Public parks, tree-lined streets and community gardens can improve life in cities. Vegetation provides shade and can absorb some rainwater, reducing pressure on drainage systems. Green spaces may also encourage physical activity and provide places for neighbours to meet.

However, the benefits are not automatic. A park that is poorly maintained, difficult to reach or perceived as unsafe may be used by few residents. Researchers therefore argue that access, lighting, maintenance and community involvement should be considered alongside the total area of green land.

Some cities have introduced small green corridors between larger parks. Early evaluations suggest these routes can make walking more attractive, although long-term results depend on local design and continued investment.
