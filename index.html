Academic Reading · Practice passage 1

Original IELTS-style passage. Read the passage and answer all five questions. Suggested time: 8 minutes.

Urban green spaces and public health

As cities expand, parks, community gardens and tree-lined streets are increasingly viewed as essential infrastructure rather than decorative extras. Researchers have found associations between access to green spaces and higher levels of physical activity. Parks can also provide places for social contact, which may help reduce feelings of isolation. However, the benefits are not distributed equally: neighbourhoods with lower incomes sometimes have fewer safe, well-maintained public spaces.

Simply building a park does not guarantee that residents will use it. Design, accessibility and maintenance matter. Paths need to be accessible to people with limited mobility, lighting can influence whether visitors feel safe, and facilities should reflect local needs. Involving residents in planning can help ensure that new spaces are used and cared for over time.

Green spaces may also help cities respond to heat. Trees provide shade and can cool nearby areas, while vegetation absorbs rainwater and may reduce pressure on drainage systems during heavy rainfall. Yet planting programmes require long-term funding: young trees need water and care before they provide substantial shade.

For city planners, the challenge is therefore not only to increase the total area of green space, but to make it accessible, inclusive and resilient. Measuring success requires attention to location, quality and community experience as well as the number of hectares created.

1. The passage says green spaces are now considered…
2. True, False or Not Given: All neighbourhoods have equal access to safe parks.
3. Name one feature that can influence whether visitors feel safe.
4. Trees can help manage heavy rainfall by absorbing…
5. The best measure of success is based only on total hectares created.

Listening · University course enquiry

Original short listening practice. Use “Play recording” to hear the scripted dialogue read aloud by your browser. You may play it twice. This is text-to-speech, not a studio recording.

Ready to play
Staff member: Good morning, Riverside Language Centre. How can I help? Student: Hello, I’m interested in the evening academic writing course. Staff member: Certainly. It begins on 14 November and runs for six weeks, every Tuesday and Thursday. Student: What time does it start? Staff member: Classes run from 6:15 until 7:45 in room 204. Student: How much is the course? Staff member: The full fee is 180 pounds, and that includes the workbook. Student: Do I need to take a placement test? Staff member: Yes, an online test must be completed before registration. Student: Great. Is there a deadline? Staff member: Please register by 7 November because places are limited.
1. What date does the course begin?
2. Which two days are classes held?
3. What time does the class finish?
4. What is included in the course fee?
5. What is the registration deadline?
